## Overview

A huge share of the real work on a redesign this size isn't writing code — it's making sure the right code gets written at all. Design decisions have to reconcile with engineering constraints, production needs a technical translator, and dozens of small calls (spacing, tokens, performance trade-offs, accessibility) need someone with enough context to make them fast instead of letting them stall. That became as much a part of my role on the Site Redesign as the section and animation work itself.

## What I did

**Owned the handoff, not just the ticket** — showed up at every stage of the pipeline, from Design and production handing off specs, to engineering picking up the build — not just receiving pre-decided work.

**Shaped how work got scoped, not just how it got built** — sat in on backlog triage and intake-process reviews, giving direct input into how requests entered the engineering queue in the first place.

**Became a go-to on performance and accessibility calls** — a standing member of a dedicated performance effort and multiple accessibility review sessions, and someone colleagues brought accessibility questions to directly rather than routing through a formal process.

**Kept design-token decisions workable at scale** — spacing and token strategy only holds up if engineering can actually implement it consistently; I was the recurring engineering voice making sure design intent and implementation reality stayed aligned across teams.

## What I learned

The hardest part of a large redesign isn't any single component — it's absorbing enough ambiguity that other people aren't stuck waiting on a decision. Being useful in that role means showing up to the messy conversations before there's a ticket to write, and being willing to make the call rather than waiting for someone else to formalize it into a request.
